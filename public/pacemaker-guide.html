Always, everywhere

The two habits that matter most
  • Carry your pacemaker ID card in your wallet
  • Wear a medical alert bracelet
  • Keep a photo of your ID card on your phone
  • Say "I have a pacemaker" before screening
  • Ask for a body scanner or pat-down, not the walk-through detector
  • Keep your ID card in your carry-on
  • Flying abroad? Ask about an international ID card
  • Arrive a little early for screening
  • Walk through entrance scanners at a normal pace
  • Don't stop or lean in the doorway gate
  • Mention your pacemaker if a scanner flags you
  • Keep your phone 6+ inches from your device
  • Don't carry it in a chest pocket
  • Use your opposite ear for calls
  • Wireless charging? Keep it 12+ inches away
  • Laptops, TVs, microwaves: no issue
  • Stand about 2 feet from an induction cooktop
  • Use the back burners when you can
  • Gas and electric coil stoves: no issue
  • Driving or riding in any car, including EVs: safe
  • Charging an EV, even a Tesla Supercharger: safe
  • Step back while it's actively charging
  • Always mention your pacemaker first
  • MRI: only if your device is "MRI-conditional," ask your cardiologist
  • TENS units and diathermy therapy
  • Routine dental work and X-rays: fine
  • Stay 2 feet from welding equipment or generators
  • Same for high-voltage transformers
  • Ask about an on-site safety test if it's part of your job
Based on guidance from Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, FDA, TSA & device manufacturers.
Confirm specifics for your exact device with your cardiologist.
